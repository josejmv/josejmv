## Problem

An institutional client needed its borrowers to pay hundreds of thousands of active obligations online, via PSE and Bre-B. The contract with the previous provider had an end date, so the replacement had a fixed date and no way back: on cutover day, the new site had to collect correctly from the very first payment.

That set three requirements: **never lose a payment even if something fails halfway**, **never block a legitimate payer**, and **pass the payment processor's certification** within the deadline.

## Architecture

The orchestrator is a NestJS backend with a Next.js payment site in front of it. The source of truth for a payment's state is the processor's signed notice, not whatever the payer's browser happens to see.

## Decisions

### The payer's return is resolved with rules, not assumptions

When the payer comes back from checkout, the state may not be settled yet. The return use case applies **five precedence rules** to decide what to show and what to query, without marking as paid anything the processor has not confirmed.

### Every webhook is verified before it touches a payment

Webhook reconciliation checks the notice's **signature** and **freshness**, using constant-time comparison so response timing leaks nothing. A stale or badly signed notice changes nothing.

<Callout title="trade-off">
  Anti-abuse lives in Redis (sliding windows, lockout and captcha), but it **fails open**: if Redis
  goes down, no payment is blocked. For a collections site, losing a legitimate payment is worse
  than letting an abuse attempt through during an outage.
</Callout>

### Personal data stays where it belongs

The backend has PII guards and a **read-only** operations console, with CSV and XLSX exports that contain no personal data. Support can investigate a payment without seeing more than it needs.

## Results

- **Certified by the payment processor**, checked item by item against its checklist, two weeks after the integration model was confirmed.
- Deployed to its environment, with a code security audit and TLS 1.2 or higher verified on the published domain.
- It is now **in pre-production testing**, waiting for the final production cutover.
